## 2. The recast, which is the easiest way to get this quarter badly wrong

Q1 FY27 is the **first quarter Commvault reports on recast definitions** announced 2026-04-28.
Customer support splits into term-based and perpetual, and **term-based support moves into total
subscription revenue**. Subscription ARR is separately recast to include enterprise support.
**Total revenue and Total ARR are unchanged.**

| | |
|---|---|
| FY26 subscription, as originally reported | $768.3M |
| FY26 subscription, recast | **$970.5M** |
| Difference, from the definition alone | **+$202.2M (+26%)** |

So the year-ago Q1 subscription comparable is **$229.3M**, not $181.7M. Anyone anchoring on the
printed figure computes roughly **+45%** growth against the guide instead of **~+15%** — a
26-point artefact with no business content. The guide of $263–265M is ~+15% off the recast base.

Because total revenue and Total ARR survive the recast intact, both are used here as the primary
series and need no rebasing.

## 6. Methods, and why they are mixed rather than averaged

Five views of the same quarter. They are **mixed**, not averaged, because they are different
stories rather than five points in one range — averaging would collapse genuine model
disagreement into false precision.

| Weight | Method | Median | sd |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35% | **Bottom-up by line** (primary) | $316.2M | 7.1 |
| 30% | **Guided line + fitted realization** | $317.1M | 7.2 |
| 10% | ARR balance × conversion factor | $320.0M | 12.1 |
| 15% | Sequential seasonality | $316.7M | 5.9 |
| 10% | y/y deceleration | $311.6M | 5.6 |

Method medians span $311.6M to $320.0M, or **$8.4M of model disagreement**. The three
best-supported methods (bottom-up, guidance realization, sequential) **agree within $2M**, which
is the real signal.

### Method 2: the guidance realization pattern

Subscription guidance midpoint vs actual, six quarters, old definition:

| Guide | Actual | Beat | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 145.0 | 158.3 | +13.3 | 1.0917 |
| 162.0 | 173.2 | +11.2 | 1.0691 |
| 168.0 | 181.7 | +13.7 | 1.0815 |
| 175.0 | 172.7 | **−2.3** | 0.9869 ← the only miss |
| 196.0 | 206.3 | +10.3 | 1.0526 |
| 205.0 | 207.6 | +2.6 | 1.0127 |

Ratio form: mean 1.0491, sd 0.0412. Dollar form: **mean +$8.13M, sd $6.50M**.

**Use the dollar form.** The beats originate in term-license revenue, recognized at a point in
time, which is also the documented cause of the single miss — term license fell $109.3M → $92.6M
into Q2 FY26. That is a dollar phenomenon driven by deal timing, not a percentage of whatever
base it happens to sit in. The recast did not touch term license; it folded in term *support*,
which is ratable. Applying the 4.1% ratio deviation to the larger recast base would therefore
**overstate** the risk, because the volatile component is now a smaller share of the line.

Centred at **+$6.0M**, below the +$8.13M fitted mean, because the last two quarters beat by only
+$10.3M and +$2.6M and this is the first quarter guided on new definitions.

### Method 3: why the ARR conversion factor is weak at a quarterly horizon

Total ARR excludes perpetual license and professional services but *includes* support on both
perpetual and term licenses, so ARR-borne revenue = total − perpetual license − other services.

```
quarterly k in FY26   1.0829   0.9920   1.0774   1.0460
                      mean 1.0496   sd 0.0417      full-year k 1.0493
```

That sd is the finding. **k swings from 0.992 to 1.083 across four quarters purely on
term-license timing**, carrying roughly $12M of noise from k alone. It is a sanity check, not a
forecast, and it is weighted 10% accordingly. Q1 FY26's 1.083 is the high-water mark and must not
be reused as a Q1 seasonal constant.

## 8. Assumption register

All recast history in §3 is **sourced**. The following are judgements, each with what would
falsify it.

| Assumption | Value | Falsified by |
|---|---|---|
| Term-based license | $112.0M (+2.5% y/y), sd 6.5 | A print outside $100–125M |
| SaaS | $100.5M (+38.7%), sd 2.0 | SaaS outside $97–104M, or SaaS ARR outside $425–435M |
| Term-based support | $56.6M (+19.0%), sd 1.0 | Outside $55–58M — would mean the term base moved |
| Perpetual support | $26.7M (−15.1%), sd 0.6 | Outside $25.5–27.5M, breaking a clean 8-quarter decay |
| Perpetual license | $5.8M, sd 1.2 | Erratic line; interval deliberately wide |
| Other services | $14.6M, sd 1.0 | Outside $13.0–16.2M |
| Beat mu (Method 2) | +$6.0M (fitted +8.13, shaded to recent quarters) | A subscription print below $263M |
| Net-new Total ARR (Method 3) | $40M | Total ARR at 6/30/26 outside $1,150–1,175M |
| Method weights | (0.35, 0.30, 0.10, 0.15, 0.10) | Nothing observable — a judgement about model quality |

**Not modelled: FX.** FY26 carried a **$29.5M reported tailwind** ($10.8M in Q4 alone). A sharp
move in April–June 2026 sits outside this interval. This is the largest known unmodelled risk.

### Corrections made during the build

Two assumptions were replaced with sourced figures once the recast quarterly tables were located,
and one was materially wrong:

- Q1 FY26 perpetual license was assumed at ~$11.5M; **actual $7.3M** (Q1 is the seasonal low for
  this line).
- Perpetual support was assumed to decline ~4%/year; **actual −14.5%**, and very consistently so.

Correcting both moved the median about $3M and roughly halved the residual guesswork.

## 9. Scoring plan

- **Resolves:** Q1 FY27 press release, 2026-07-28 08:30 EDT.
- **Benchmark to beat:** guidance at face value — subscription midpoint $264M + residual $47.1M =
  **$311M total**, treated as N(311, 6.5).
- **Scores:** CRPS on total revenue; pinball loss at p10/p50/p90; record the realization ratio and
  refit the beat distribution before Q2.
- **Failure condition:** this beats the benchmark only if the beat pattern holds. If Commvault has
  genuinely tightened its guidance process, the naive benchmark wins and the fitted realization
  distribution should be rebuilt with the new observation rather than defended.

### Consistency with the FY27 annual guide

Multiplying Q1 by four is the wrong check, because Q1 is seasonally the weakest quarter. Use the
observed Q1 share of the full year:

| Q1 share of FY | Implied FY27 total |
|---|---|
| 23.8% (FY26 actual) | $1,327M |
| 24.5% | $1,291M |
| 25.0% | $1,265M |

FY25 share was 22.6% and FY26 was 23.8%, drifting up as ratable SaaS flattens the seasonal curve.
Against guidance of **$1,300–1,310M**, that band **straddles the guide rather than contradicting
it**. So this Q1 forecast does not require the annual guide to be wrong — a useful check, because
a quarterly call that silently implies a large annual beat usually contains an error.
